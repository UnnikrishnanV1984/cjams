Intake Process for Delinquency Complaints

The purpose of the Intake Interview is to determine whether the Juvenile Court has jurisdiction over the alleged offense and, if so, whether judicial action is in the best interest of the public or the youth or both.

After determining jurisdiction, the Intake Officer must make one of the following decisions:

  1. Close the complaint at intake with a warning
  2. With the consent of the victim, the youth and the youth’s parent(s)\guardian, place the youth on Informal Supervision for up to 90 days, unless otherwise extended by the Court
  3. Authorize the filing of a petition and forward the complaint to the Office of the State’s Attorney

If a petition is filed and the Court makes a finding of delinquency, the Court may,

  1. Place the youth on probation under conditions of supervision that the Court, or any agency designated by the Court, deems appropriate
  2. Commit the youth for placement in a public or private facility
  3. Commit the youth to a training school or similar institution

The Intake Officer must make a decision within 25 days of the date that the complaint is received by the Department of Juvenile Services.

At the Intake Interview, the juvenile charged has the right to have an attorney. If the juvenile wishes to have an attorney one must be engaged by the juvenile.

At any point in the Intake Interview, the juvenile has the right to remain silent.

If a petition is authorized and formal Court action is pursued, any information received by the Intake Officer during the intake process cannot be used against the youth at the hearing to determine whether the youth committed the delinquent act alleged.

If the Intake Officer denies authorization to file a petition the victim, the arresting officer and the person or agency that filed the complaint or caused it to be filed has a right to appeal this decision to the Office of the State’s Attorney within 30 days of notification being sent of that decision.

If the complaint alleges the commission of a felony, or a misdemeanor handgun violation, the State’s Attorney can also reverse the Intake Officer’s decision to deny authorization to file a petition or to pursue an informal adjustment.

If you do not understand any part of the information contained in this form, the Intake Officer will explain it to you at the time of the Intake Interview.
